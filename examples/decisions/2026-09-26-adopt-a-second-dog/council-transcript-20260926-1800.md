# Council transcript: adopt a second dog this winter, or wait a year?

*An invented example. Run 2026-09-26 18:00. Advisors and reviewers on Sonnet, the chair on Opus.*

## The framed question

Alex has one dog, seven years old, anxious when the house is empty for more than a few hours. A rescue has a younger,
calm dog available now. Alex works in an office three days a week. Options: adopt this winter, wait a year, or foster
first. Adoption is hard to undo. Success: two settled dogs and a calmer first dog.

## The advisors

**The Contrarian.** Picture December. Two dogs, two leads, dark at seven in the morning and dark again at five. That is
the part nobody pictures in September. And an anxious dog does not always calm down with company; sometimes it gets a
second thing to worry about.

**The First Principles Thinker.** Who is this for? If it is for the first dog's nerves, the question is whether company
helps this particular dog. If it is for the household, it is a different question with a different answer. Decide
which question you are answering before choosing an answer.

**The Expansionist.** A second dog could be the best thing for the first one. Dogs that settle together often make
empty days easier, and the household gets a second companion for the years ahead.

**The Outsider.** I would ask the first dog. Can you borrow a friend's calm dog for a weekend and watch what happens?

**The Executor.** Fostering is a trial run with an exit. Ask the rescue about their foster programme this week.

## Peer review (anonymised)

Four of five reviewers named the Executor's answer strongest. Three named the Expansionist's the biggest blind spot
(it assumes company helps). All five said the council had not asked about the older dog's health, which changes the
cost picture.

Mapping: A = the Expansionist, B = the Outsider, C = the Contrarian, D = the Executor, E = the First Principles Thinker.

## The chair's synthesis

**Where the council agrees.** Nobody knows yet whether company helps this dog. That is the real unknown.

**Where the council clashes.** The Expansionist expects company to help; the Contrarian expects a second worry. Both
happen in real households.

**Blind spots the council caught.** The older dog's health, and what a long vet bill would do in the same season.

**The call.** Foster a dog for six weeks this winter before deciding. It tests the real worry, which is the first dog,
not the new one.

**The one thing to do first.** Call the rescue and ask whether their foster programme takes a household with a
resident dog.

*This is advice from five viewpoints of one AI model. The decision is Alex's.*

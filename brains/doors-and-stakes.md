# Doors and stakes: how far to walk

What it covers: the triage at the fork, which decides whether a question needs a council, a premortem, or nothing.

- A two-way door can be undone; a one-way door cannot (or only at real cost). Ask "Can you undo it?" first. (from the
  decision-policy skill, after the Type 1 / Type 2 framework)
- Two-way and low stakes: just decide, at about 70% of the information you wish you had. No council. (from
  decision-policy)
- Two-way and high stakes: a council is worth it; if the same kind of call keeps coming back, it is a trail-marker
  candidate. (from decision-policy)
- One-way, any stakes: a record every time, and the full council (and a premortem on the chosen plan when stakes are
  high). Never a standing marker. (from decision-policy, gate 2)
- A call becomes a trail marker only when all four gates pass: it recurred three or more times, it can be undone, the
  stakes are bounded if the rule is sometimes wrong, and the inputs are stable. (from decision-policy)
- Every marker carries an "Escalate-if" line: when one of those is true, the marker does not apply and the question
  comes back to the fork. (from decision-policy)
- Legal, medical, tax and investment questions get the options and the questions for a licensed professional, not
  advice. (from Bryn's design, DESIGN.md section 3)
